# Curation — tracking sheet

**Objective.** This site serves two readers: MIT Media Lab admissions and hiring reviewers.
The Media Lab reader looks for a research question, method rigor, systems thinking, and
tool-building. The hiring reviewer looks for polish, shipped outcomes, institutional
credibility. The selection rule for everything below: *diagrams and field evidence over
renders; the system over the object; the instrument over the illustration.*

**Narrative thread.** One question — *how do you make an invisible urban system visible,
legible, and participatory?* — pursued with escalating instruments:

> **drawing** (studio work) → **embedding** (Living Heritage, Koliwadas) →
> **auditing** (ICLEI–NIUA) → **building** (Climate Code, Perspectives, Sama)

The reading order of the site (`next:` links) should trace this arc.

**Anything marked `[confirm]` is a fact I could not verify from the archive — fill it in,
don't let it ship as-is.**

### 08 · eco-machine — NEW PAGE

```yaml
title: eco-machine
question: can a city's most abused infrastructure — its open drains — be redesigned as living systems?
desc: mumbai nallahs · living infrastructure
topics: [water, community]
role: research · systems design
period: "[confirm]"
context: mumbai · nallah network
status: studio project
```

**lead:**
> Mumbai mismanages 93% of its waste, and much of it ends in the nallahs — the open
> drains that stitch the city together and poison it at the same time. Eco-machine
> treats the drain as a system to repair rather than a wound to cover: a chain of
> living infrastructure that cleans as it flows.

**body:**
> A nallah is the most honest drawing of a city — everything the city discards,
> in one continuous section. This project walked one drain end to end and documented
> what the city prefers not to see: who lives against the water, where the waste
> enters, what still grows.
>
> The proposal borrows its name from ecological engineering: an eco-machine is a
> sequence of living systems — wetland cells, root zones, settling pools — each one
> a treatment step. Strung along the drain, they turn a linear dump into a working
> landscape, and the people beside it from its victims into its operators.
> [confirm: proposal specifics against the booklet before shipping]

**method:**
1. **walk** — the drain documented end to end, on foot, in photographs.
2. **trace** — where waste enters, who lives against it, what survives in it.
3. **retrofit** — living treatment chained along the flow, sized to the neighbourhood that runs it.

**captions:**
- hero (`EcoMachine.jpg`) — *"the eco-machine — treatment as a landscape, not a plant."*
- `06` (nallah photos) — *"the evidence — 93% of mumbai's waste is mismanaged; this is where it goes."*
- remaining 3–4 after booklet triage.

### 09 · water urbanism — NEW PAGE

```yaml
title: water urbanism
question: can a five-hundred-year-old irrigation system plan a city better than its masterplan?
desc: aahar-pyne · wetland urbanism
topics: [water]
role: urban design studio
period: "[confirm]"
context: "[confirm city] · south bihar's aahar-pyne system"
status: studio project
```

**lead:**
> The aahar-pyne — south Bihar's centuries-old network of channels and catchment
> basins — treated water as the region's first infrastructure and settlement as its
> second. This studio reverses the modern order of planning: the water system is
> drawn first, and the city is negotiated around it.

**body:**
> Masterplans usually treat water as a constraint — a line to build up to, a hazard
> to drain away. Here the historical water logic *is* the plan: pynes set the street
> grid, aahars become the civic commons, the flood line is the first line drawn and
> the last one crossed.
>
> The plan is phased the way water moves — wetland first, food systems next,
> streets and built fabric last — so that at every stage the city works, and at no
> stage does it work against the water.

**method:**
1. **read** — the aahar-pyne system mapped as living infrastructure, not heritage.
2. **align** — channels set the grid; catchments set the commons.
3. **phase** — wetland → food → street → fabric, in the water's order.

**captions:**
- hero (`masterplan.jpg`) — *"the plan — wetlands as civic space, the flood line as the first line drawn."*
- `aahar pyne.jpg` — *"the precedent — five hundred years of water infrastructure, read as a plan."*
- `phase 02` / `phase 03` — *"phasing in the water's order — the city arrives last."*
- `section.jpg` — *"the section — where the street ends and the water begins is a design decision."*
- `wetland.jpg` / `street.jpg` / `food.jpg` — *"three systems, one plan — wetland, street, food."*
- `view 01.jpg` — *"the city, living with its water rather than beside it."*

### 10 · generative techniques — NEW PAGE

```yaml
title: generative techniques
question: can a neighbourhood be designed as rules rather than a plan?
desc: rule-based urbanism · eight typologies
topics: [data]
role: computational urban design
period: "[confirm]"
context: ahmedabad · [confirm precinct — gandhi ashram edge]
status: studio project
```

**lead:**
> Instead of drawing one masterplan, this studio wrote the rules that could draw
> many. Density is allocated by relationship — highest at transit stops and street
> edges, lowest near the ashram, the swale, the treatment plant — and eight
> distinct typologies are generated from the same rule set.

**body:**
> A plan fixes outcomes; a rule set fixes intentions and lets outcomes vary. The
> difference matters in a city that will not hold still. Encoding the design as
> relationships — what should be dense, what should be quiet, what must stay
> open — makes the reasoning inspectable in a way no rendered masterplan is: you
> can disagree with a rule precisely.
>
> This is the earliest project on this site where the design object is a system
> rather than a place — the thread that later becomes tools.

**method:**
1. **encode** — design intent written as explicit, arguable rules.
2. **generate** — eight typologies produced from one rule set.
3. **judge** — the variants compared against what the rules couldn't see.

**captions:**
- hero (`density distribution.jpg`) — *"density as a rule, not a shape — highest at transit, lowest at the ashram."*
- `typology diag.jpg` — *"the rule set."*
- `typology-T1…T8` as one grid — *"eight neighbourhoods from one intention — the grid is the argument."*

### 11 · utopias to heterotopias — NEW PAGE

```yaml
title: utopias to heterotopias
question: what happens when the perfect grid meets the lived city?
desc: ideal plan vs. existing settlement
topics: [community]
role: urban design studio
period: "[confirm]"
context: "[confirm site]"
status: studio project
```

**lead:**
> Every utopia is drawn on empty ground; every real city refuses to be empty. This
> studio starts from the ideal plan and lets the existing settlement talk back —
> the grid dissolving exactly where life already had other arrangements.

**body:**
> The masterplan-collage carries the whole argument: an orthogonal fabric arriving
> from one edge, an older order holding the other, and the interesting city
> happening in the seam. Rather than resolving the collision, the project designs
> the seam itself — staggered nodes where the two logics trade.
> [confirm: brief specifics before shipping]

**method:**
1. **impose** — the ideal grid, drawn without apology.
2. **listen** — where the existing city contradicts it, recorded not erased.
3. **dissolve** — the plan gives way precisely where it loses the argument.

**captions:**
- hero (`masterplan-collage.jpg`) — *"the seam — where the drawn city meets the lived one."*
- `community-diagram.jpg` — *"the other order — what was already there, drawn with equal care."*
- `staggered-nodes.jpg` — *"the negotiation — nodes where the two logics trade."*
- `section.jpg` — *"the section through the seam."*
