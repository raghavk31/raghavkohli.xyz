## 00 four cities

The Indonesia–Malaysia–Thailand Growth Triangle asked for green city action plans for four
Malaysian local governments at once, and the point of doing four together was the method. A
state secretariat reading Kota Bharu next to Penang needs the same sections in the same order,
built from the same kinds of data: a city profile from GIS, a climate risk and vulnerability
assessment on the ClimateResilientCITIES method, a GHG inventory on the Global Protocol at
BASIC level for 2017 to 2022, and a list of priority sectors and quality-infrastructure projects
a bank can pick up. **The instrument is the deliverable; the four books are it, run four times.**

## 01 profile

Every profile begins with maps made from the city's own data — boundaries and roads from the
council and PLANMalaysia, population from the census, land cover from Sentinel-2 at ten metres —
so that the first thing a reader sees is a city they recognise, drawn consistently. Penang Island
is 307 square kilometres, 794,313 people in the 2020 census, 45 per cent forest, densest in
George Town, Jelutong and Ayer Itam; the two land-cover maps five years apart show the built-up
edge climbing the hill.

## 02 services

Before the risk and the carbon, the services. For each city, four diagrams — energy, waste,
water, wastewater — on one drawing system: the agency responsible, the source, the supply and
the demand, and the number that tells you where the city stands. Kota Bharu is 70 per cent
coal-fed and 2 per cent sewered. Langkawi pipes more than half its water from the mainland.
Padawan runs on Sarawak's hydropower and uses five times the electricity per person that Kota
Bharu does, and 76 per cent of its households are on septic tanks. Penang collects all of its
800 tonnes of waste a day and sends it across the bridge to a landfill.

Sixteen drawings, one legend. **Put side by side, they are the comparison the secretariat
asked for, before a single table.**

## 03 risk

The risk assessment reads the hazards — heat, flood, sea-level rise, landslide — against the
systems they land on. Penang's surface runs 20 to 34 °C, and 37 °C at 76 per cent humidity
feels like 55; the mean maximum has risen 0.9 °C since 1981. Rainfall reached 2,552 mm in
2022 and the north-east district's low ground floods. The maps put the clinics, schools,
substations and bus routes on the hazard, and the hotspot map is where the systems fail
together — which is where the money in section D should go first.

## 04 inventory

The inventory is the ledger. Penang Island used 25.58 million gigajoules of energy in 2022 and
emitted 4.72 million tonnes of CO₂e — 5.82 tonnes a person, against a national 10.16 — with
manufacturing first, then commercial and residential buildings, then transport and waste. Kota
Bharu, with half the people and no heavy industry, is 0.76 million tonnes and 1.86 a person.
Langkawi's emissions are 38 per cent transport and grew 44 per cent in five years; Padawan's are
46 per cent transport and a fifth waste. The same table, four cities, and the differences are
the plan.
