## 00 the city

A climate action plan is only as good as the picture of the city it starts from. This one
starts from a single municipality in a single year — Vadodara, 2021–22 — a city of two and
a half million on the Vishwamitri, with a development plan already in force and a set of
utilities that keep it running. The plan is written for the people who run those things,
in the units they use.

## 01 the atlas

Before the plan proposes anything, it draws. Twenty maps at ward level, in three passes.
First the **infrastructure** as it stands: wastewater, drainage, waste, transport, tree
cover, parks, fire cover, health, air. Then the **hazards**: the slums inside the heat,
the flood-prone areas and the diseases that follow them. Then the cross: heat laid over
water supply, flood over water supply, heat over the transport network, flood over the
roads and the emergency services that depend on them — and one composite of every
hazard against every fragile system.

## 02 the inventory

The maps say where; the inventory says how much. Every activity number has a named
source — the electricity utility, the oil and gas companies, the corporation's own fleet
and plants, the railway, the airport — and the sum is a number the city can be held to:
**3.04 million tonnes of CO₂e in a year**, 1.25 tonnes per person. Vadodara's profile is
the reverse of Ahmedabad's: transport first at 36%, homes second at 32%, industry only
14%.

Five years of the same accounting, 2017–18 to 2021–22, give the trend: 16.7% up, 4.2% a
year, with the pandemic dip in between. That is the business-as-usual line the rest of
the plan argues against.

## 03 the pathway

Three lines to 2070. Business as usual, rising to thirty million tonnes. The
**progressive action scenario** — every scheme and target already on the books at the
state and the centre, pushed as far as they go — which cuts 59.9% from BAU and leaves
twelve million tonnes on the table. And the **net-zero pathway**, which extends each of
those actions until the remaining line meets the target: 46.8% below BAU in 2030, 62.7%
in 2050, 88.2% in 2070.

Buildings do most of the work — 78.9% of BAU emissions come from stationary energy, and
efficiency plus renewables in homes, offices and industry can halve the BAU line on their
own. The corporation proposes two hundred electric buses by 2025 as a start.

## 04 strategies

Eight sectors, each with one goal, a handful of strategies, and targets by decade — a
zero-waste city by 2030, 100% wastewater treated and reused, the National Clean Air
Programme met. My part of the book begins here: the strategies were drawn before they
were tabulated, so that a department head could see the piece of city each one changes.

## 05 actions

The action sheets are the part of the plan that gets picked up. Each sector opens with
its 2021 baseline and the gaps the audit found; each action follows with its target, the
entity that implements it, the financing mode, the indicative cost and timeline, and a
resilience score. A sheet is what a department takes to a budget meeting.
