## 00 the island

Majuli is a river island of about 350 square kilometres in the Brahmaputra, and shrinking: the
river has taken more than half of it in a century and takes a little more each monsoon. What it
holds is unusual — the satras, monastic institutions of neo-Vaishnavite Assam, some of them four
centuries old; Mising villages of stilt houses; mask-making, boat-building, pottery without a
wheel. The winter school spent two weeks documenting the island's culture and its settlement:
the satras and their key plans, activity maps traced along the lanes, and one household per
student, measured.

## 01 the compound

Mine was in Garmur. A compound is not a house; it is a set of things a household keeps at the
right distances from each other. The house on its platform, its verandah toward the lane. The
granary on its own stilts, the bathing place and the water, the kitchen garden, the cattle. The
boundary is bamboo and areca, and the lane is the neighbours'. The plan is drawn as it was paced
— every tree that shades something is in it.

## 02 the house

The chang ghar is a house on a floor above the flood: a bamboo-and-timber platform on stilts
about a metre and a half up, the sleeping areas and the kitchen in a row along it, a verandah
where most of the day happens, a ladder. The materials are keyed at the edge of the drawing —
brick where a wall has been rebuilt, corrugated tin and country tiles over the newer rooms,
thatch over the older. **The section is a flood record.** The height of the stilts is the height
the water has reached; the drawing was made to keep that number.
