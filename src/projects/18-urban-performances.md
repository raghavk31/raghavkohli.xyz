## 00 the vav

Ahmedabad's walled city has dozens of stepwells, most of them dry, protected, and shut. The
Amritvarshini Vav at Khadia is one of the smallest: a flight of steps down to a well, a colonial
building over one side, the fort wall and a bastion on the other. Its section is an
auditorium's — steps facing an arch — and the performance took that literally.

## 01 set-up

A white sheet hung from the parapet and pulled square across the lower arch. A projector on the
top landing, a laptop on the step below it, a cable to the nearest plug. Three people to hang the
cloth, one to aim the light. The whole set-up fits in a rickshaw, and the monument is untouched.

## 02 the screening

From six the sheet went up; by eight the steps were full. The film mattered less than the fact
of it — that a place the city keeps by keeping people out can be re-activated for two hours by
letting them in. The last image thrown onto the sheet was the vav itself, projected onto its own
arch. **The performance was the argument: a monument's future use is a matter of permission, not
construction.**
