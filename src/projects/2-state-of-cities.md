## 00 the unit

Cities are where emissions are made and where the data is worst. The report
starts with the world's cities as the unit of account — fifty footprints on one map,
a handful of them larger than most countries — because that is the scale at which
anything gets decided, and the scale at which almost nothing is measured well.

## 01 the nation

India's curve runs up to 2019 and then splits: business as usual, or what the
cities could do. The national commitments — 500 GW of non-fossil capacity, a
billion tonnes cut by 2030, net zero by 2070 — are made in Delhi and delivered,
if at all, by municipalities.

## 02 the instrument

CSCAF 2.0 is the framework that turns a city into a rating: five sectors, five
stars, from "yet to consider" to "showcased successful implementation." It is the
first instrument that lets fifteen Indian cities be read against each other at
all. Ten of them were already acting on it.

## 03 fifteen cities

Every city got the same spread: a profile, a star rating, two donuts, a column of
measures. The template is the point. It is the first time fifteen Indian cities
could be read side by side on one instrument.

Read across them and the instrument shows its seams. The inventories span
**2012 to 2021**; a person in Panaji is charged 3.5 tonnes, in Thane one. And one
city's total and its per-capita figure cannot both be true — the book's own sum
only works if you pick the wrong one.

## 04 the path

Assess, commit, plan, implement, monitor, revise. The book draws it as a loop.
The loop needs one thing most cities do not have: a data layer they can maintain
themselves — which is where the next five years of this work went.
