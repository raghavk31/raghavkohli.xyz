## 00 site

Surat is the first ally to Mumbai on the western coast — diamonds, textiles, and migrants
from every state to work them. Its answer to housing them has been towers, isolated from the
mixed-use fabric the city has always had, or redevelopment on the outskirts, away from the
work. Maan Darwaza is where many of them actually live: a dense neighbourhood on a creek that
is at once the settlement's edge, its dump and its washing ground.

The studio's frame was the history of mass housing — linking and stacking, Tietgenkollegiet
and its Hakka precedents, the utopias — and its demand was that the housing be designed **for
at least two communities on the same ground**, as a live-work habitat rather than shelter.

## 01 mapping

Before any unit was drawn, the blocks were mapped five ways: building heights, land use,
gender density, sound, community. The maps are dull on their own and sharp together — where
the women are, where the noise is, which lane belongs to whom. Alongside them, sketches of the
spaces each community had already made for itself: a shrine in a stair, a tailor's front step,
plants on a ledge. **What people had built without a plan set the brief for the plan.**

## 02 linking and stacking

The unit is a one-bedroom module. Everything else is how it links and stacks. The study models
ran through rings, chains and open courts — how many modules make a cluster, where the stairs
land, how a terrace is left over for the unit below. The two- and three-bedroom units are the
same module extended, and the space left open or covered beside them is **deliberately
unfinished: room for the inhabitant to expand over time.**

## 03 clusters

The number that mattered was seven or eight — units to a cluster, small enough for one
community to live with itself, arranged around a court it shares with no one else. The
clusters meet at staggered nodes, and the nodes are where the staircases are, so that two
communities climb the same stair. The commercial lane cuts the site into three: a garage yard
for those who work in the garages, an inter-community open ground, and the daily-needs edge.

## 04 the plan

Two hundred units for five hundred people, on 4,100 square metres of existing built area. Four
unit types — a single migrant's room, a 43 square metre four-person dwelling, two two-person
variants — from one module. The rings thin as they rise, so the roofs are the open space the
ground cannot spare, and the whole steps down toward the creek.

## 05 the model

The final model is card and mount board. The clusters read as what they were meant to be: not
blocks but rings of terraces, each court open to the sky, each roof someone's yard.

## 06 section

The section is where the argument lives. Shops under, dwellings over; the terraces and courts
as the life between; the creek at the end of it, made an edge worth reaching. Every utopia is
drawn on empty ground. This one is drawn on Maan Darwaza's, and leaves the last move to the
people who will live there.
